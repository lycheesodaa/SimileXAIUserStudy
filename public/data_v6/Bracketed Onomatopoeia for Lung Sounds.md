
> [!faq]
> These onomatopoeia descriptions are intended to complement the simile set. They use sound-mimicking words and phonetic representations to capture the acoustic character of each lung sound class.
>
> Unlike similes ("like X"), onomatopoeia phrases render the sound itself in text — useful for prompting TTS/audio generation models that respond to phonetically evocative language. Variations in intensity, rhythm, or pitch can be achieved by adjusting the phrasing (e.g., "rapid krrk-krrk-krrk" vs. "slow, spaced krrk … krrk … krrk").

### 1. Crackles (Rales)
> _Discontinuous, non-musical popping, snapping, or bubbling sounds spanning fine (high-pitched, dry) and coarse (low-pitched, wet) crackles._
- [rapid, tiny, snapping] pop-pop-pop!
- [faint, dry, soft] crick-crick-crick!
- [miniature, thin, airy] snap-snap-snap!
- [gentle, rapid, high-pitched] crr-crr-crr!
- [soft, irregular, high-frequency] tic-tic-tic!
- [deep, rolling, bubbling] blub-blub-blub!
- [heavy, wet, pouring] glug-glug-glug!
- [moist, drawn-out, reverberating] krackle-krackle!
- [rumbling, low, wet, resonant] brrr-brrr-brrr!
- [heavy, staccato, loose, shifting] rattle-rattle-rattle!

### 2. Wheezes (Sibilant)
> _High-pitched, musical, continuous sounds often heard on expiration. Caused by narrowed airways._
- [thin, sustained] wheeeee!
- [high, musical, boiling] eeee!
- [reedy, undulating] shhhhh!
- [narrow, whistling, cutting] fweeeee!
- [vibrating, buzzing, harmonic] zeeeeee!
- [continuous, shrill, forced] sweeee!
- [quavering, modulating, spasmodic] wheee-wheee!
- [piercing, musical, undulating] eeeoh!
- [tense, buzzing, high-frequency, sustained] zzzzz!
- [musical, gliding, warbling] fwee-fwee!

---
### 3. Rhonchi (Sonorous Wheezes)
> _Low-pitched, snoring, or moaning sounds. They imply obstruction of larger airways and often clear with coughing._
- [deep, rumbling, resonating] snorrr-snorrr!
- [low, drawn-out, vibrating] groannn!
- [throaty, idling] rrrumble-rrrumble!
- [sonorous, fluctuating] mmmmm!
- [reverberant, humming, low-frequency] brronnn-brronnn!
- [coarse, droning, sustained] rrrrr!
- [low, nasal, reverberating, obstructed] honk-honk!
- [muffled, clearing] grrronk!
- [buzzing, baritone, vibrating, labial] bzzzz!
- [rolling, undulating, low-pitched] rrr-owww-rrr!

---
### 4. Stridor
> _Loud, high-pitched, crowing sound usually heard without a stethoscope during inspiration. Indicates upper airway obstruction._
- [harsh, crowing, forced, narrowed] krrrow!
- [sharp, piercing, metallic, inspiratory] krrk-krrk!
- [loud, barking, echoing] rawk!
- [strained, scraped, painful, narrowed] eeerk!
- [grating, strident, tight, inspiratory] squeeek!
- [desperate, struggling, obstructed] hrrk-hrrk!
- [metallic, resonating, tracheal] screech!
- [crowing, short, snapping, half-closed] crow-crow!
- [high, rasping, abrasive] cchhhh!
- [piercing, clattering, loud, audible] shrieek!

---

### 5. Normal Lung Sounds
> _Clear, rhythmic breath sounds with no added noises. Indicates healthy, unobstructed airways._
- [soft, even, unimpeded] whoosh!
- [quiet, airy, smooth, rising, fading] shhh!
- [steady, gentle, clean, open] fwooo!
- [smooth, expanding, effortless, clear] haaa!
- [low, rhythmic, breezy] swooo-swooo!
- [calm, regular, unhurried, tidal] hoo-hoo!
- [faint, gliding, unresisted] hushhh!
- [soft, clear, laminar, non-turbulent] sss!
- [quiet, even, rhythmic] fwsh-fwsh!
- [placid, full, deep, unobstructed] ahhhh!
